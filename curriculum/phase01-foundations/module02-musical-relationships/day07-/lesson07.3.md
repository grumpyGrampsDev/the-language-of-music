# Day 7.3 — The Jobs Move

## Learner's Question

> **If every note has a job, why do those jobs change when the song changes?**

---

## Looking Back

You have discovered two ideas that work together. First, music naturally creates a sense of home. Second, every other note takes on a different role because of its relationship to that home. Today we're going to test an assumption you may not even realize you've made.

Does a note always have the same job?

Or can its job change?

---

## A Thought Experiment

Imagine an actor performing in different plays. In one story, they're the hero. In another, they're the villain. In a third, they're comic relief. The actor hasn't changed. Only the story has. Their role changes because of the story they're part of.

Music works much the same way. A note doesn't carry a permanent musical role. Its role depends on the musical "story" surrounding it.

---

## Exploration: Moving Home

Choose a note anywhere on the guitar. Let it become home. Spend a minute exploring around it, just as you did yesterday.

Notice which notes feel settled.

Notice which create tension.

Notice which seem eager to return home.

Now stop.

Choose a completely different note somewhere else on the fretboard. Forget the first home. This new note is now home. Repeat the same exploration. Listen carefully.

The notes you are playing haven't changed. The guitar hasn't changed. Your ears haven't changed.

But something else has.

The roles have shifted. A note that once felt settled may now feel restless. A note that once created tension may now feel comfortable. The notes stayed the same. The jobs moved.

---

## Reflection

Ask yourself:

- Which notes changed the most?
- Did any notes feel completely different than they did before?
- Was it the note that changed...
- ...or your understanding of where home was?

This is one of the most important questions in all of music.

---

## Discovery

A note does not carry a permanent musical job. Its role depends entirely on where home is. When home moves...

...the jobs move with it. This is why the very same collection of notes can create completely different musical experiences. Meaning is never attached to the note alone. Meaning is attached to the relationship.

---

## Naming the Discovery

This idea appears throughout music. A melody can be moved to a different starting note. A song can be played in a different key. An entire piece can be transposed higher or lower. The notes may change. The pitches may change. But if the relationships stay the same, the musical roles move together. Understanding this is far more valuable than memorizing individual notes.

You're beginning to think about music the way musicians do.

---

## Practice

Choose three different home notes.

For each one:

- Establish the home by playing it several times.
- Explore the surrounding notes.
- Listen for rest, tension, motion, and release.
- Pay attention to which feelings stay familiar and which ones move.

Don't try to memorize anything. Instead, notice the pattern. Every time home changes...

...the musical landscape reorganizes itself.

---

## Reflection

Before these lessons, you may have thought of notes as fixed musical objects. Now you've discovered something deeper. The notes stay the same. The relationships stay the same.

But meaning moves with home.

That realization opens the door to understanding scales, keys, harmony, improvisation, and composition—not as separate topics, but as different ways of organizing musical relationships.

---

## The Language of Music Principle

> **A note's identity never changes. Its role changes whenever home does.**

---
